# Encoding decisions

Deliberate decisions of character-encoding detection (`_encoding.decode_text` and text detection — shared by TXT, MD, CSV, HTML and mail parts). Split off from [extractors.md](extractors.md);
the rule is the same: do not revert the decisions in this table. To change one, change its pinning test first and write the reason in the same row.

| Decision | Why | Pinned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strict CP949 decoding comes before the statistical guess | charset-normalizer misjudges short CP949 as UTF-16 and the like | `test_office.py::TestCsv::test_decodes_cp949_and_sniffs_delimiter` |
| UTF-16 without a BOM is judged text by null-byte positions + strict decoding + a printable check. Spaces (Zs — NBSP, U+3000) and format characters (Cf — U+200B, BOM) count as printable too (`_encoding._printable`) | A note saved without a BOM by a Windows tool was rejected as binary. `str.isprintable` rejects NBSP, the full-width space and ZWSP, so a single such character got a note or CSV rejected | `test_text_html.py::test_decodes_utf16_without_bom`, `test_core_text_and_mail.py::TestUtf16WithoutBomAndSpaceCharacters` |
| When strict CP949 decoding fails, the text is read with replacement characters and a warning only when there is at most 1 damaged byte per 200 characters and it is mostly Hangul. A double-byte character is replaced whole (`codecs.register_error`, process-global). A statistically guessed encoding whose later part does not decode is not discarded either but read with replacement | A CP949 note with its last character cut became Thai and Japanese characters throughout. With fewer than 10 Hangul characters, only when the last byte is a Hangul lead byte (0xB0–0xC8), the front decodes strictly and every non-ASCII character is everyday Hangul (2 or more) (`_korean_cut_at_the_end`; 0 verdict changes over 155,000 short texts in other encodings) | `test_text_html.py::TestDamagedLegacyKorean`, `test_spreadsheet.py::test_csv_in_cp949_with_a_cut_last_character_is_read_as_korean`, `TestShortDamagedLegacyKorean` |
| Right after strict UTF-8 decoding fails (before the declared charset and CP949), 'almost UTF-8' (only the last character cut + 2 or more complete multi-byte characters, or at most 1 replacement character per 200 characters with 10 or more multi-byte characters per replacement) gives `utf-8` with the replacement suffix (`_encoding._REPLACED`) + a warning. Known accepted gap: cut text with at most 1 complete multi-byte character goes to the statistical guess (indistinguishable from ASCII text ending in a single CP949 Han character) | Short cut UTF-8 was garbled whole as ptcp154 or cp875. 0 verdict changes over 40,000 short texts in other encodings | `test_text_html.py::TestAlmostUtf8` |
| Text detection: NUL at most 0.1% (at least 1 allowed) + UTF-8 is text. Otherwise it is text when the rest without the NULs decodes strictly as UTF-8 or CP949 and has no control bytes that do not occur in text (`detect._BINARY_CONTROL`) (regardless of the NUL share; tar excluded). In TXT/MD, 2 or more consecutive NULs become the same number of spaces (fixed-width fields) and single ones are removed. UTF-16 without a BOM tolerates `\f\v ESC` and odd length; with under 10% NULs and UTF-8, or CP949 once the NULs are removed, it is not UTF-16 | A single null or a single form feed got text rejected as binary. CP949 text was rejected for one null, exports that pad fields with NULs were rejected whatever the encoding, and short CP949 text was garbled as UTF-16. Executables and pictures have other control bytes and are still rejected | `test_text_html.py::TestTolerantTextDetection`, `TestLegacyTextWithNulBytes`, `test_detect.py::test_rejects_unknown_binary`, `TestTextWithNulBytes` |
| When strict CP949 decoding works it is CP949 even without Hangul. But when every non-ASCII byte is right in front of a Latin letter, it is Windows-1252. For the damaged-CP949 verdict only the 2,350 characters of KS X 1001 count as Hangul. Known accepted gap: CP949 that has only the form `A햏B`, EUC-JP that has only kana | ㎡, ℃ and ※ were garbled into Johab/Big5 characters, and `Company’s` into `Company뭩` | `test_text_html.py::TestLegacyKoreanWithoutHangulAtTheFront`, `TestWesternText` |
| At the statistical-guess stage, when non-ASCII never runs 3 bytes or longer and the coherence of the top guess is not higher than that of the Windows-1252 candidate, it is Windows-1252. Known accepted gap: Central European text such as Hungarian where they tie | Among tied candidates the alphabetically first (cp1250, cp1257, cp437) was picked and `naïve` became `naļve` | `test_text_html.py::TestWesternText` |
| The charset declared in HTML (meta, XML declaration) comes after strict UTF-8 and is used only when it decodes strictly. A Windows-1252 or ISO-8859-1 declaration comes after strict CP949 (a declaration servers attach by default). EUC-KR → CP949, shift_jis → cp932, gb2312 → gbk. When decoded with replacement, HTML and XML spreadsheets warn too | A document declaring shift_jis was garbled as `cp949` with the replacement suffix, without even a warning. A Korean CP949 page labelled ISO-8859-1 has to be judged by content | `test_text_html.py::TestHtmlDeclaredCharset`, `test_spreadsheet.py::test_xml_spreadsheet_with_a_damaged_byte_reports_the_replacement` |
| The statistical encoding guess for HTML is made on the text without tags (`decode_text(markup=True)`; on the whole when non-ASCII occurs only inside tags) | Short Windows-1252 HTML was read as CP775 because of the markup characters and `10€ café` became `10Ć cafķ` (the same text as .txt was right) | `test_text_html.py::TestWesternHtmlWithoutAUsefulCharset` |
| UTF-32 with a BOM (LE, BE) is text like UTF-16 (`_encoding._BOMS`, LE before the UTF-16 LE BOM; detect takes it out of binary detection through `unicode_bom_codec` and looks for HTML in the text decoded with that codec). Known accepted gap: UTF-32 without a BOM is binary | The BE BOM (`00 00 FE FF`) starts with nulls and was rejected, and LE passed by accident because it decoded as UTF-16 and the nulls were removed | `test_text_html.py::TestTolerantTextDetection::test_utf32_with_bom_is_text`, `test_utf32_with_bom_is_decided_by_content_when_the_extension_says_nothing`, `test_utf32_csv_with_bom_is_a_table` |
| When the declared charset is a 7-bit escape codec (ISO-2022-JP family, ISO-2022-KR, UTF-7; `_encoding._SEVEN_BIT_CODECS`), it is used after the BOM and before strict UTF-8. Only when every byte is ASCII, it decodes strictly and no ESC or unprintable character remains (both mail part charsets and HTML meta arrive as `declared` of `decode_text`) | Being 7-bit, strict UTF-8 decoding accepted it and it became `$B2q5D…(B` with the escapes removed. Text declared UTF-7 with a raw `+` mixed in (`a+b`) fails strict decoding and follows the old order | `test_core_mailboxes_and_charsets.py::TestSevenBitEscapeCharsets` |
| TXT (including mail and mbox bodies, `text.extract_text`) has terminal control sequences removed whole from the decoded text (CSI `ESC [ parameters final character` — colour `ESC[31m`, erase line `ESC[2K`, cursor `ESC[?25l`; OSC window titles ending in BEL or `ESC \`; DCS, PM, APC; character set `ESC(B`; two-byte commands `ESC c`, `ESC 7` etc.; `text._TERMINAL_SEQUENCE`). An OSC without an end is not removed (so it does not swallow the text after it). Deliberate: MD stays as written (`extract_markdown` reads `_plain_text` directly) | In a TXT of a saved terminal log only the ESC was removed (clean_text) and `[31m`, `[0m` stayed attached to every word. When only CSI was removed, the `(B` of `tput sgr0` and the window title `]0;제목` remained | `test_core_header_tables_cell_code_and_terminal_codes.py::TestTerminalColorCodesInText`, `test_core_round44.py::TestTerminalEscapesOtherThanCsi` |
| Text behind a BOM that does not decode strictly is read with replacement and reported like every other replacement decoding (`utf-16(replaced)`) | A lone surrogate became `�` with no warning | `test_audit_html_text.py::TestDamagedTextBehindAByteOrderMark` |
| A statistical guess of UTF-16 or UTF-32 for a sample without a NUL byte is taken only when it decodes into plausible CJK or Hangul text: at most one character in 50 outside CJK ideographs, kana, CJK punctuation, Hangul syllables, full-width forms and common punctuation, and Han mixed with Hangul only in a text of at least 8 characters that is at least 30% Hangul (Korean with hanja) (`_encoding._plausible_best`, `_plausible_wide_text`); otherwise the next guess is used. The order of the steps, the declared charset included, is unchanged | Text in those encodings has NUL bytes as soon as it holds an ASCII character, so without any it can only be text such as Japanese or Chinese with no space or line break, which must stay UTF-16. Short broken UTF-8 (`ab=ZZé` + a cut character) was read as UTF-16 and became `慢㵚嫃꧃` without a warning. Refusing every Han+Hangul mix dropped `大韓民國의首都는서울이다`; on 2,000 random and broken short inputs the rule takes 2 as UTF-16 where the bare statistical guess took 34 | `test_audit_web_fixes.py::TestNoWideEncodingGuessWithoutNulBytes` |
