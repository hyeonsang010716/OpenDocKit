# PDF decisions: resource limits, page boxes and attachments

Decisions of the PDF extractor (`src/opendockit/extractors/pdf.py` and the `_pdf_*.py` modules beside it) about resource limits (time budget, decompression, font ranges, overdrawn copies, text-box grouping, rendering, pdfminer logging), page boxes and attachments. Split off from [pdf.md](pdf.md)
because of its size limit; the rules are the same: do not revert the decisions in this table. To change one, change the pinning test first and write the reason in the same row.

## PDF resource limits

| Decision | Why | Pinned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pages beyond the PDF time budget (default 60 seconds) go to pdfium | A 102-page PDF of vector shapes went from 184 seconds → 62 seconds, with the text preserved | `test_pdf.py::test_time_budget_reads_remaining_pages_with_pdfium` |
| If the number of line pieces obtained by joining a page's text chunks (pdfium `count_rects`) (`_text_line_pieces`: joined when they overlap vertically by half or more and the horizontal gap is at most the height) exceeds 2,000, that page is read without text-box grouping (`boxes_flow`) and a warning is issued. The limit value is the owner's decision. The ruled-grid judgment (`_is_ruled_grid`) uses the raw chunk count | Grouping grows quadratically, so a page of 4,700 scattered characters took 54 seconds and 3.2GB. The maximum over 993 pages of public PDFs is 1,900. Chrome places Hangul separately per character, so chunks are 71% of the character count (LibreOffice 13%), and an ordinary two-column paper page (about 2,900 characters, 2,136 chunks → 103 line pieces) hit the limit and the column order was mixed (round 34). The cost of grouping depends on the number of text boxes, and there are no more text boxes than line pieces. Scattered characters stay separate and are still caught (2,500 → 2,431) | `test_pdf.py::test_page_of_thousands_of_scattered_text_pieces_is_read_without_box_grouping`, `test_pdf_resource_limits.py::TestGlyphByGlyphText` |
| Text-box grouping is bounded per page, its cost counted as the square of a layout's box count: the page's own layout may cost up to 1,000,000 (`_PAGE_GROUPING_COST`) on its own — and so may its regrouping after joining line pieces (`_rejoined_lines`), which replaces the first grouping — and the layouts of all Form XObjects drawn on the page (`all_texts`) share another 1,000,000 (summed, reset per page read and re-read), so a page runs at most three groupings of up to 1,000 boxes each (the body, the body again after joining pieces, its forms together). The bound limits the pairs of boxes pdfminer keeps in its heap (the square of the box count), not the time: for every pair pdfminer also looks through the plane for a box lying between the two, which costs more the further apart the boxes lie and the more of them lie on top of each other; a layout beyond its allowance is ordered as pdfminer orders it without text-box grouping (`_group_few_textboxes` in place of `group_textboxes`, only during conversion: the boxes are analysed and numbered vertical right to left, then horizontal top to bottom and left to right — the same order as `boxes_flow` None) and its page gets the warning of pages read without text-box grouping. Such a page, read again for a width of separately placed text, is read again from the same document with the page's cost reset, so the same layouts stay ungrouped. Lines of a layout that lie above, below or more than 1pt right of the visible area are not read (`_beyond_view`): every character of such a line is outside the view and already dropped, so the line was dropped anyway, but only after picking the kept characters at its height and scanning the removed ones (8,000 characters right of the page on the rows of 40,000 small ones, 21 KB: 27 s without, 1.9 s with). Known accepted gap: a line left of the visible area is still judged by that scan (a wide kept character could be centred inside). Known remaining cost (a single slow page, which the time budget cannot interrupt): pages under every bound still take 10 s and more — see the measurements | Grouping keeps a heap of every pair of boxes: 1,000 separate boxes took 2 s, 1,500 6 s, 2,000 14 s, and a 68 KB page whose 6,000 characters lie far off the page (each its own box) 122 s inside one page, where the time budget does not look (main: 141 s). The pdfium line-piece estimate did not see them — pdfium keeps positions in single precision, so characters 20pt apart at x = 2e9 fell on one spot. Over the 566 PDFs of the review corpus the largest layout has 848 boxes (2.1 s), the next 320, so no page of it changes. Measured on an Apple M4, one grouping of up to 1,000 boxes takes 2 s when the boxes lie 30pt apart on an A4-sized page and about 4 s when they are scattered over a 6,900pt page, so a 12 KB page under every bound — 990 characters scattered over the page, 990 more in a form and one line drawn in two pieces, so that the body is grouped twice — takes 10 to 12 s (the same on main). A layout whose line grouping gave up (next row) is not grouped at all, whatever its box count. Per layout (1,500 boxes) a 4.3 KB form of 1,400 boxes drawn 15 times still spent 54 s, one layout of 1,450 boxes 4.1 s, and a page found crowded only while being read was re-read from the ungrouped document that had not been opened (the whole document failed as corrupted). Per page, the 983 pages of the corpus spend at most 848² = 719,104 (2.0 s to read the page), the next 102,400. One allowance shared by everything on the page charged the body for the discarded first grouping of pieces and for forms laid out before it (pdfminer analyses forms first), so pages within the bound lost their column order and got the warning; a page that meets the bound only when read again also gets the warning | `test_audit_pdf_text.py::TestTooManyTextBoxes` (the allowance of forms per page read and re-read: `::test_the_forms_of_a_page_are_not_charged_for_those_of_the_page_before`, `::test_a_page_read_again_is_not_charged_for_the_forms_of_the_page_read_before_it`; lines beyond the view: `::test_lines_beyond_the_view_are_not_matched_against_the_characters_of_the_page`) |
| pdfminer's grouping of text lines into text boxes (`group_textlines`) runs with a work allowance during conversion (`_group_textlines_within`): the objects in the plane cells it looks through for each line's neighbours and the cells that search spans (`Plane.find`, `_find_counted`) plus the lines it gathers per box and 10 more for each distinct line it adds (`uniq`, `_uniq_counted`) — 20,000,000 (`_LINE_GROUPING_WORK`) for the page's own layout, the same shared by the Form XObjects of the page (reset per page read and re-read). Past it every line becomes a text box of its own (linear), the boxes of that layout are ordered without text-box grouping (`line_spent_layout`: such lines lie within the line margin of each other, and boxes lying on top of each other are what pdfminer groups slowest — 500 of them on a page, 1.8 KB, took 8 s and 1,000 in a form, 29 KB, 52 s under the text-box bound, against 0.6 s and 2.0 s on the version without the allowance; 0.3 s now) and the page gets the warning of pages read without text-box grouping. pdfminer's planes are also given wider cells, doubling the 50pt size, when the objects handed to an empty plane span more than 20,000 cells (`_PLANE_CELLS`, `_extend_coarsely`): the cells only index the objects, so only the order `find` gives them in can change, and only on such planes | Lines within the line margin of each other all find each other and every line re-adds the whole box it joins: characters drawn alternately at two places rising 0.1pt every two took 4.3 s at 2,000 (5.4 KB), 16 s at 4,000 and 62.5 s at 8,000 (main 18.8 s at 4,000), and one paragraph of 6,000 lines spent 10 s here, inside one page with no warning (`boxes_flow` None does not avoid it and the result is few boxes, so the text-box bound never acts). An object is filed under every cell it covers: one character of size 200,000 in a form with a large BBox (859 bytes) took 8.6 s, two characters 300,000pt apart 18 s. Over the 566 PDFs of the review corpus the largest layout does 3.5 million of line-grouping work (0.1 s), p99 27,000; 20 million is about 0.5 s; an A0 poster spans 3,300 cells | `test_audit_pdf_text.py::TestTooMuchLineGrouping` (each kind of work: `::test_each_kind_of_work_counts_towards_the_allowance`; the forms of a page: `::test_the_forms_drawn_on_a_page_share_one_allowance`, `::test_the_forms_of_a_page_start_with_a_whole_allowance`; lines left a box each: `::test_lines_left_ungrouped_are_not_grouped_as_boxes`) |
| Whether a kept layout line has removed characters under it (copies drawn on top, and whether one is a shadow copy) is answered by an index of the removed characters (`_CopyIndex`: 32pt columns by left edge, each sorted by vertical middle, stopping at the first found, a second index for shadow copies), with exactly the answer of the scan of the removed characters at the line's height (`_chars_at_line_level` with the left-edge filter); a line over 4,096 columns is answered by the scan, and characters whose left edge or middle is not a finite number are at no line's level | Stacked text drawn repeatedly keeps 3 copies of each character and removes the rest, and with every line its own box every kept line scanned every removed copy at its height: a 4.5 KB page of 1,500 characters drawn 30 times took 54 s, 1,500 drawn 10 times 8.9 s (main 60 s), 4,000 drawn 10 times 15 s — now 2.8 s, 0.8 s and 2.5 s | `test_audit_pdf_text.py::TestCopiesOfStackedText` |

Counting them rejected a table-of-contents document whose result is 2,741 characters as exceeding a limit of 8,223 characters (round 36) — the running check must never be larger than the character count of a full conversion (the `exceeded` row in [core.md](core.md)) | `test_pdf_toc_and_checkboxes.py::TestDotLeaderPageNumbers::test_long_dot_leaders_do_not_count_against_the_limit_before_they_are_shortened` |
| The per-page running check of the character limit (`_countable_chars`) counts text read sideways (`page.sideways`) and form values (`page.form_values`) as well as lines and tables, with the same `(cid:n)` and leader-dot exclusions | Both are always written at the end of the page, yet were left out, so a document whose text is all rotated by 90 degrees (200 pages under a 5,000-character limit) was analysed page after page until the 60-second time budget stopped it | `test_audit_pdf_text.py::TestLimitCountsSidewaysTextAndFormValues` |
| The running check does not count edge lines that may be erased as repeated lines (the edge area and the two top and bottom rows), but does count a line that would fall short of the repetition criteria (half of all pages, 2/3 of the odd or even pages, at least 3 pages) even if it appeared on every remaining page to the end (the `limit` page) (`_EdgeTally`, counted on the text with digits masked and spaces removed). If the setting is not to erase repeated lines, everything is counted. Known accepted gap: for a little over the first half of the pages, edge lines are still not counted | In a slide-type document with one or two lines per page every line is an edge line and was counted as 0 characters, so 75 pages of lecture slides (5,493 characters) were analysed to the end even at 10% of the limit, and a 1,500-page document took 7–11 seconds (round 48). The repetition key (`_row_key`) masks only some digits, so lines with the same key also have this same text, and nothing is overcounted (the `exceeded` invariant). The slack up to the criteria shrinks more slowly than the number of remaining pages, so checking it only against the last page is correct wherever the conversion stops | `test_pdf_round48.py::TestEdgeLinesThatCannotRepeatCount` |
| PDF rendering bitmaps are at most `_MAX_RENDER_PIXELS`; on a page that exceeds it only the picture area is rendered | An 8000pt page took 9.4GB | `test_image.py::TestResourceBounds::test_pdf_figure_on_a_huge_page_renders_only_its_own_area` and 1 more |
| A page with more than 50,000 PDF drawing objects (`_MAX_PAGE_OBJECTS`, including those inside forms) gets pdfium plain extraction without pdfminer + a warning | A single page of 240,000 Form XObjects took 65 seconds and 4.6GB (it has only 280 text pieces, so it passed the existing check). | The decompression limit (`max_uncompressed_bytes`, the same value as for ZIP·HWP and the same `DocumentTooLargeError` saying the document becomes too large when decompressed) is applied to all PDF streams (`_InflateBudget`). The whole document is rejected (not just the page skipped — the limit is a document total). It is charged in two places: every stream decode of pdfminer (`PDFStream.decode` replaced with `_decode_within_budget` — including object and cross-reference streams; a `ContextVar` only during conversion), and the content streams and resources of a page before pdfium opens it (`_charge_page`). The size is measured by inflating 1MB at a time (`_inflated_size`). Picture XObject and attachment streams are not measured. Exceptions wrapped by pdfplumber are also checked before falling back to pdfium plain extraction (`_check_budget`). Known limitation: documents pdfminer cannot open at all and that are read with pdfium only, and the LZW·RunLength stages, are not measured | The content stream of a 760KB PDF (800MB when inflated) was inflated whole in the pdfium survey and again in pdfminer, using 2.49GB and 3.5 seconds → rejected at 90MB and 0.5 seconds. pdfium inflates first during the page survey, so blocking only pdfminer is useless. Object streams are inflated when pdfminer looks up an object, and treating that failure as 'structure analysis failed' makes pdfium inflate them again. Pictures: large scanned documents would hit the limit, and they are not inflated for reading text (rendering is covered by `_MAX_RENDER_PIXELS`) | `test_pdf_resource_limits.py::TestDecompressedStreamBudget` |
| A page with more than 1,000 text chunks (`_GRID_MIN_TEXT_PIECES`) and at least half as many lines and shapes (pdfium paths) (`_is_ruled_grid`) is also read without text-box grouping. No warning is issued. Known accepted gap: text outside the table on such a page is in top-to-bottom order | A page of a ruled table with 1,210 cells was below the 2,000 threshold and spent 3.6 seconds per page on grouping, so a 30-page document went over the time budget from page 18 and became plain extraction without tables → 30 pages in 16 seconds, tables on every page. | Copies of the same character drawn at the same place (character, font, size, position to 0.1pt; a NaN value is keyed as None) more than 3 times (`_MAX_OVERPRINTS`) are discarded as soon as the layout object is created (wrapping pdfplumber `render_char`, only during conversion).
