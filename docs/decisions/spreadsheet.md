# Spreadsheet decisions

Deliberate decisions of the Excel and CSV extractors (`spreadsheet.py` for XLSX with `_openpyxl_reading.py` and `_xlsx_parts.py`, `xls.py`, `xml_spreadsheet.py`, `csv_text.py`; number formats in `_sheet_format.py`, rows → tables in `_sheet_layout.py`). Split off from [extractors.md](extractors.md); the rule is the same:
do not revert the decisions in this table. To change one, change its pinning test first and write the reason in the same row.
The decisions about CSV delimiters, quotes and line verdicts (`extract_csv`) are kept separately in [csv.md](csv.md). Decisions shared by CSV and Excel, such as value formatting, are in this file; splitting tables within a sheet, table title rows and filling horizontal merges are in [spreadsheet-tables.md](spreadsheet-tables.md).
Cell values that do not fit the format, pictures in cells, form controls, SmartArt on sheets and default-password XLS are in [spreadsheet-cells.md](spreadsheet-cells.md).

Which tables, title rows and columns the sheet has is still decided on all the rows read, so the drawing does not change the text cut at the limit, and a sheet within the limit is drawn whole. Deliberate: those rows are the ones read before the counted cell text passed the limit (`_collect_rows`), not the whole sheet, so the cut text of a sheet can lay out otherwise than the front of its full text (a column or title row decided by rows after the stop; 56 of 830 cuts of adversarial CSVs before this row). Reading on past the stop to settle the layout would bring back the cost this row removes. What the drawing wrote is then counted (`_append_sheet`), so a later sheet has only what is left: a workbook past the limit stops at that sheet, and the hidden-sheet warnings of the sheets after it are no longer given (as when its cell text alone passes the limit). A cell's line of nothing but quote marks (`>`) counts toward the limit, unless the cell holds a picture (`_cell_length`). A table row is built from the cells it has when it is written (`markdown._render_rows`), not padded to the widest row first.
