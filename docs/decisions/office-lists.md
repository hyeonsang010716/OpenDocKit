# Office numbering and list decisions

Deliberate decisions of the DOCX and PPTX extractors about numbering and lists (automatic numbers, list levels, line breaks inside items, lists inside cells). Split off from [office.md](office.md);
the rule is the same: do not revert the decisions in this table. To change one, change its pinning test first and write the reason in the same row.

## DOCX / PPTX numbering and lists

| Decision | Why | Pinned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DOCX numbers come from w:start, w:lvlText, startOverride and the w:lvl of lvlOverride (an unknown numFmt is Arabic digits). decimalZero, decimalEnclosedParen, decimalEnclosedFullstop and decimalFullWidth take their Word-specific shapes (01., ⑴, ⒈, １; ⑴ and ⒈ are digits from 21 on). A w:isLgl (legal style) level shows the numbers of the preceding levels in Arabic digits too (`NumberLevel.legal`) | '제%1조' came out as '1.', '01.' and '⑴' as '1.' and '1', and legal-style '1.1.' under 'I.' as 'I.1.' | `test_office.py::TestDocxObjects::test_numbering_uses_start_and_level_text`, `TestOfficeReadingOrderAndListMarks::test_zero_padded_parenthesized_full_stop_and_full_width_numbers`, `TestOfficeLineBreaksNestedFieldsAndMetadata::test_docx_legal_numbering_shows_every_level_in_arabic_digits` |
| A line break inside a PPTX text-box paragraph (a:br, Shift+Enter) stays a line. List-item paragraphs go through `pptx._item_lines` (the same rule as DOCX `_list_item_lines`; in addition `1. `/`1) ` lines (up to two digits) are sub-items too — a `1) ` line becomes `- 1) ` (the `list_item_prefix` form); `12. 31. 기준`, where another `N.` follows, is a date and so a continuation line). Lines of non-list paragraphs stay as they are. The slide title is the section head, so it is merged with spaces (**deliberate**). Known accepted gap: an a:br in the middle of a sentence splits the line too | '분석 항목: 1. 수익성 비교 … 2. 안정성 비교 …' became one line (table cells and notes kept their lines). A `1) ` line only got indentation, which `clean_text` removed (list lines are only `-`, `*`, `1.`), and a date line became a sub-item | `test_pptx_*` in `test_office.py::TestOfficeLineBreaksNestedFieldsAndMetadata`, `test_office_symbols_fields_notes.py::TestPptxHandTypedLinesInsideAnItem` |
| DOCX number counters are per abstractNum and restart only when a num with startOverride first appears. Style numbering follows basedOn, with the level from w:lvl/w:pStyle; numStyleLink resolves to the styleLink definition; a numPr with only ilvl and no numId is the style's list at the paragraph's level. Empty numbered paragraphs are counted too (not output; deleted paragraphs and the original position of moved paragraphs w:moveFrom excluded). For a numbered paragraph wrapped in an empty content control (showingPlcHdr) the prompt text is left out but the number is counted. Bullet and no-number levels also pass through the counter so that deeper levels restart at 1 | Counting restarted at 1 for every numId, inherited numbers were missing, numbered lists became bullets and later numbers moved up. A number under a bullet item continued from the previous item, so a 4-step procedure in a real handover document came out as 6–9. **Aligned with LibreOffice and the spec, not the Word screen** | `test_office.py::TestDocxNumbering`, `test_office_revisions_and_numbering.py::TestDocxPlaceholderNumberedParagraph` |
| DOCX `w:lvlRestart` is the deepest higher level (from 1; ECMA-376 17.9.11 — `NumberLevel.restart_after`) that makes this level count from 1 again: 0 never restarts, N only when level N or a shallower one appears. A missing or out-of-range value (its own level or above) is the default (any shallower level). HWP/HWPX always use the default | It was not read, so '제N조' (Article N) continuing across chapters went back to '제1조' in every chapter (the same article number appears twice and article references are wrong). **Could not be confirmed on the Word screen (by the spec)** | `test_office_revisions_and_numbering.py::TestDocxLevelRestart` |
| A DOCX no-number level (numFmt none) is not a bullet: the number of that level is empty text (`format_number` "NONE"); when nothing remains in the number text (lvlText) it is a paragraph without a mark, and when something remains (numbers of higher levels, literals) that text is the number (same as the Word screen). The counter still counts | In a document that made the explanatory paragraph under a number a numFmt none level, the explanation became a `- ` item and read like a list item | `test_office.py::TestDocxNumbering::test_level_without_a_number_format_is_a_plain_paragraph` |
| When the definition a DOCX number reference points to does not exist (no numbering.xml, no w:num for the numId, no such level (w:lvl) in the number definition), it is an ordinary paragraph without a mark (`docx._list_prefix`). It does not pass through the counter either. numId 0 is no number as before | Word draws no number, yet a bullet `- ` was attached and an ordinary paragraph read like a list item (a generator's document with the number definitions removed). 0 output changes over 17 example DOCX files. **Could not be confirmed on the Word screen** | `test_office_revisions_and_numbering.py::TestDocxNumberReferenceWithoutADefinition` |
| A level definition (w:lvl) inside DOCX `w:lvlOverride` changes the number text and format only for that w:num. The counter keeps counting per abstractNum and is not restarted from the definition's w:start — only startOverride restarts (at that value, when it first appears) | Only startOverride was read, so '제3조' came out as '3.'. Restarting from w:start whenever there was a definition turned the `B`, `C`, `4` of files saved by Word (Apache Tika `testWORD_override_list_numbering.docx`, POI `NumberingWOverrides.docx` 'Test 4'; paragraph text = number on the Word screen, confirmed by Tika's test) into `A`, `B`, `3` (the earlier decision 'restart from the definition's w:start' was changed with the owner's approval) | `test_office.py::TestDocxNumbering::test_level_override_with_its_own_definition_replaces_the_level`, `test_level_override_restarts_only_with_a_start_override`, `test_level_override_can_turn_a_numbered_level_into_bullets` |
| When a lower-level (indented) DOCX/PPTX number is not of the form `1.`, a bullet is prefixed, as in `- a. text`, `- 1.1. text` (`_shared.list_item_prefix`). Top-level numbers (`제1조`, `가.`, `1)`) stay as they are | `markdown._LIST_LINE` recognizes only `-`, `*` and `1.` lines as lists, so the indentation of 'a.', '1)' and '①' sub-items was removed and blank lines appeared around them. The same form as HTML letter numbers. Prefixing a bullet to top-level paragraphs is noise | `test_office.py::TestDocxNumbering::test_sub_items_with_numbers_other_than_digit_period_keep_their_level`, `TestPptxAutoNumbering::test_numbers_follow_the_scheme_and_count_per_level` |
| PPTX automatic numbers (a:buAutoNum) are counted per level within a text box: a deeper level does not interrupt the numbers of a shallower one; when a shallower level appears the deeper ones restart; an unnumbered paragraph of the same level (including an empty one) or a different number shape restarts; empty numbered paragraphs are skipped; startAt is the first value of the continuing numbers. The bullet is looked up in the order paragraph → the shape's a:lstStyle → a:lstStyle of the layout/master placeholder (the master's p:txStyles is not looked at). A top-level paragraph with a direct a:buNone gets no `- ` even in a body placeholder, and with an a:buChar/a:buBlip bullet it gets `- ` even in a text box. Known accepted gap: numbers in table cells and speaker notes are not added (0 real samples) | Numbers were not read, so body placeholders came out with '- ' and text boxes without a marker. Opening and closing sentences read like list items. The counting rules were aligned with the PDF rendered by LibreOffice. **Could not be confirmed on the PowerPoint screen** | `test_office.py::TestPptxAutoNumbering`, `TestOfficeReadingOrderAndListMarks::test_text_box_paragraphs_with_a_bullet_character_or_picture_are_list_items` |
| DOCX list levels: while the same list (numId) continues, by level (ilvl) (inheriting the shift of the previous item); where the list changes, by the on-screen left indent (style → number level → the paragraph's w:ind, overriding per attribute; when leftChars is non-zero, that × 2 twips): the same level as the previous item (difference within 50 twips) or the level below it. A non-list paragraph closes only items indented further than itself (continuation text aligned with the item text does not close them; body text at the margin closes all), and a hand-typed `- `/`* ` line in continuation text that was not closed is a list line one level below the remaining item (each line joined with w:br too; other lines stay as they are). Headings, cells and headers are counted separately. When there is no indentation anywhere, levels only | In real proposals and review requests that made sub-items with a different numId, the items under '1.1 지표명' went to the top level and the detail items under '기후위기 심화' came out at the same level. Indentation is not looked at within one list, so single-list output is unchanged. A hand-typed `- ` paragraph came out at column 0 after a deep item and read like a top-level item. Other text is not indented because `clean_text` removes leading spaces (to prevent code blocks). The precedence of indentation for style numbering (a style's w:ind may come before the number's) was not examined. **Aligned with the indentation values in the XML, not the Word screen** | `test_office.py::TestDocxListDepthCheckBoxesAndLongHeadings::test_item_of_another_list_nests_under_the_item_it_is_indented_from`, `test_indentation_counts_the_paragraph_and_style_indents_and_body_text_ends_the_nesting`, `test_hand_typed_dash_paragraph_continuing_an_item_is_indented_under_it` |
| DOCX list levels: a list that comes back next to an item of another list with the same indentation uses the shift that list used last (`_list_offsets`) and is never placed deeper than the neighbouring item. A non-list paragraph with indentation 0 (or none) closes all preceding items. Lists inside a text box are counted separately, like cells and headers (`_own_lists`) | When a numbered list and a bullet list alternated at the same indentation, each change of list inherited the neighbouring item's level and it went one level deeper per chapter (a staircase up to 8 levels, even in documents where every indentation is 0). A '- ' paragraph at the margin after a list with indentation 0 became a sub-item, and a margin paragraph in a text box closed the preceding items | `test_office.py::TestDocxListDepthCheckBoxesAndLongHeadings::test_alternating_lists_at_the_same_indent_do_not_staircase`, `test_margin_dash_paragraph_after_a_list_without_indent_stays_at_column_0`, `test_text_box_in_a_list_item_does_not_close_the_items_before_it` |
| A line break (w:br) inside a DOCX numbered or bulleted item paragraph is not merged into a space (`_DocxRenderer._list_item_lines`): the first line is the item; a line starting with a bullet such as `- `, `* `, `·`, `○`, `▪` is an item one level down (`- ` is added when missing); other lines stay as continuation lines (leading spaces are removed by `clean_text`); blank lines are left out. Inside a cell lines are `<br>` and sub-items use `cell_level_mark`. Merging line breaks of heading paragraphs is unchanged (row above). Known accepted gap: a w:br in the middle of a sentence aligned by hand splits the line too (same as paragraphs outside lists) | Hand-typed sub-bullet lines were merged into one line, so 'Activity - (SKI) 신규 Pipeline 발굴 : … - (SKI) 탄소배출권 투자 유치 : …' in a real review request became a single 600-character line. | `test_office.py::TestDocxListDepthCheckBoxesAndLongHeadings::test_line_breaks_inside_a_list_item_keep_their_lines` |
| Lower levels of a list inside a DOCX table cell are `└ `, `└─ ` (`_shared.cell_level_mark`, same as HWP/HWPX). No `- ` is prefixed to numbers inside a cell (`└ 가. 설계`) | Leading spaces of lines inside a cell are removed by `markdown._clean_cell`, so sub-items were flattened | `test_office.py::TestDocxListDepthCheckBoxesAndLongHeadings::test_list_levels_inside_a_table_cell_are_marked_like_hwp` |
| The level of a PPTX table-cell paragraph (a:pPr lvl) is also `└ `, `└─ ` at the start of the line (`pptx._cell_paragraph_text`, same as DOCX/HWP cells). Bullets and numbers are not added (cell text is read without bullets) | Paragraphs were joined without levels and sub-items were flattened. Leading spaces inside a cell are removed by `markdown._clean_cell` | `test_office_cells_and_notes.py::TestPptxTableCellLevels` |
| The level of a PPTX speaker-notes paragraph (a:pPr lvl) is `└ `, `└─ ` at the start of the line (`_shared.cell_level_mark`). Numbers are not added (accepted gap of the automatic-number row). After the notes body placeholder, the text of non-placeholder text boxes on the notes page is appended too (the inside of group shapes is not looked at) | Reading with `text.splitlines()` lost the level. Leading spaces of quote-block lines are merged by `clean_text`, so indentation cannot convey it. Notes of files where LibreOffice exported the notes as a text box were missing | `test_office_cells_and_notes.py::TestPptxSpeakerNoteLevels` |
| Hangul and Han counting numbers (HWP/HWPX 일/一; DOCX koreanCounting, chineseCounting, ideographDigital, japaneseCounting; PPTX ea1) are counting words past 10 as well (`_shared._counting_number`: 십일, 이십, 백일, 천십; 十一, 一百零一, 一千零一十), and Arabic digits from 10,000. DOCX koreanDigital goes digit by digit (일영, 일일). 가나다 and ㄱㄴㄷ cycle through 14, the 15th being 가/ㄱ again (deliberate) | From 11 on it switched to '11'. The shapes are the same as on the LibreOffice screen (11 DOCX numFmt kinds × 1–111 and 1001–9999), and the 가나다 cycling is the same as LibreOffice (`bRecycleSymbol`). **Could not be confirmed on the Word or Hangul screen** | `test_office_round45.py::TestNumbersPastTheTable` |
